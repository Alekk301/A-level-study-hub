# AI assistance and authorship

This project was built with extensive help from ChatGPT, Codex and Claude. I estimate that AI
generated or substantially assisted **60–70% of the code and educational content**.

That disclosure is intentional. The value I claim is not “I typed every line alone.” It is that I
identified a real problem, directed a product for real users, made and revised decisions, checked
the result, and am taking responsibility for learning and maintaining the system.

## My contribution

I, Đức Anh Lê (Alex Le):

- chose the problem: scattered revision material for my classmates and me;
- chose the audience and the four-subject CAIE A-Level scope;
- decided to replace the original frontend and use a familiar learning-site structure;
- prioritised structured notes, paper discovery and a low-friction experience without accounts;
- worked on the problem of collecting, pairing and connecting question papers and mark schemes;
- tested the paper-browsing flow and checked whether notes were detailed and relevant to exams;
- gathered classmate feedback;
- accepted the request for saved highlighting as a product requirement;
- chose to keep the service free;
- decide what is published and accept responsibility for errors.

## AI-assisted work

AI tools contributed to:

- much of the React, TypeScript, CSS and Worker implementation;
- refactoring the original static site into the current routed architecture;
- code review, debugging and test generation;
- converting and expanding educational material into structured note data;
- documentation structure and wording;
- design iteration and accessibility suggestions.

Some architecture and deployment decisions were collaborative: I supplied goals and constraints,
while AI suggested and implemented technical approaches.

## Verification standard

AI-generated output is not accepted merely because it compiles. Before I claim a part as understood
or reliable, I aim to:

1. trace its inputs, transformations and outputs;
2. explain why the chosen approach is safe and appropriate;
3. run relevant automated checks;
4. manually test the user flow;
5. compare educational claims with the syllabus, mark schemes or another reliable source;
6. record limitations instead of hiding uncertainty.

The learning plan in [LEARNING-GUIDE.md](LEARNING-GUIDE.md) is part of that responsibility.

## What I will not claim

- I will not claim that this was unaided programming.
- I will not claim authorship of Cambridge papers, external PDFs or credited diagrams.
- I will not claim analytics figures that I have not exported and dated.
- I will not claim that every note is error-free or teacher-approved.
- I will not use generated wording as a substitute for my own voice in an application essay.

## Why the project is still meaningful

Software projects involve problem selection, requirements, iteration, verification, trade-offs and
communication as well as code entry. AI assistance changes the evidence an evaluator should use:
the strongest evidence is my understanding, decision record, tests, response to feedback and ability
to make a new change independently.
